# KineTrolley: Kinetic Energy Harvesting System

## Project Overview

KineTrolley is a simulation project that demonstrates how a trolley can harvest kinetic energy from wheel movement, convert it into electrical energy, and store the energy in a rechargeable battery.

The stored energy is then supplied through a simulated power interface to a low-power electrical load representing a shopping mall application.

## Problem Statement

Imagine pushing a trolley at a supermarket. The wheels keep turning as we move, but all that movement energy is simply wasted. What if we could turn that wasted energy into electricity? Our project does exactly that by using the trolley’s movement to generate and store electricity in a battery, creating a simple and useful power source for trolley users.

## Objectives

* To simulate electricity generation from trolley wheel movement.
* To demonstrate battery energy storage.
* To simulate the transfer of stored energy to a shopping mall electrical load.
* To explore the potential of kinetic energy harvesting in commercial buildings.

## Features

* Animated trolley and rotating wheels.
* Adjustable trolley speed.
* Adjustable generator efficiency.
* Simulated battery charging.
* Mall power connection and disconnection controls.
* Real-time energy dashboard.
* Simulated power output and energy consumption.

## How to Run

1. Download or clone this repository.
2. Open the project folder in Visual Studio Code.
3. Open `index.html` in a web browser.
4. Alternatively, use the Live Server extension in VS Code.
5. Use the simulation controls to start the trolley and connect the simulated battery to the mall load.

## Energy Conversion Process

Mechanical Energy → Electrical Energy → Battery Storage → Power Converter → Mall Electrical Load

## Important Note

This project is a conceptual simulation for educational purposes. Its power-generation values are illustrative and are not based on measurements from a physical prototype.

A real system connected to a building electrical supply would require suitable power conversion equipment, electrical protection, and approval from qualified professionals.

For a student prototype, a separate low-voltage DC load is recommended.
